**She doesn't pretend.** The single most important thing about Bella is what she does when she's *not* certain: she stops, takes the caller's name and number, and flags your team to ring back. A moment of honesty keeps a booking; a confident wrong answer loses one.

## Uncertainty is a feature, not a failure

Plenty of automated phone systems are built to always say *something*. That's how you end up with a table booked for the wrong night or an order sent to the wrong kitchen. Bella is built the other way. When a request falls outside what she can confirm — an unusual dietary question, a complaint, a supplier, a booking rule only a manager should bend — she hands it over cleanly instead of improvising.

## What the handover looks like

The caller doesn't hit a dead end. Bella captures who they are and what they wanted, and that lands with your team as a callback to make, with the context attached. So the call still becomes a lead you can win — it just gets a human where a human is needed.

For the everyday calls, she's fully self-serve: a table, a time, a takeaway order, your hours. The handover path only exists for the edges, and those edges are a small share of a typical night.

## Accents and noisy lines

Australia's dining rooms are multilingual and its phone lines aren't always clean. Bella is tuned for Australian accents and local place and suburb names, and she'll ask a caller to repeat themselves rather than assume — the same thing a good host does. If a line is genuinely too broken to be sure, that again becomes a callback, not a guess.

## Why this protects your reputation

A restaurant lives on trust. A guest who's told "yes, 8pm Saturday" and turns up to no table remembers it — and tells people. By refusing to bluff, Bella keeps the one promise that matters: what she confirms is real. That's worth more than answering every question in the moment.
