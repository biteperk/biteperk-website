The single biggest worry owners have about letting an AI answer the phone is also the easiest to put to rest: **you don't change your number, and you don't rip out your phone system.** You forward calls to the AI host's line — and you decide exactly when.

This guide walks through the three common setups in Australia. It takes about five minutes.

## First, decide *which* calls to forward

You have three sensible options. Most venues start with the second.

- **Forward everything.** Every call goes straight to the AI host. Best for venues where the phone reliably goes unanswered during service.
- **Forward when busy or unanswered** (recommended to start). Your staff still pick up when they can; anything that rings out after a few rings rolls over to the host. You catch the misses without changing how the floor works.
- **Forward after hours only.** Your team handles daytime calls; the host covers nights, early mornings, and closed days.

You can change your mind any time — forwarding is a setting, not a commitment.

## Telstra mobile or landline

For most Telstra services, the call-forwarding codes are:

- **Forward when unanswered:** dial `*61*` followed by the host number, then `#`, and press call.
- **Forward when busy:** `*67*` + host number + `#`.
- **Forward all calls:** `*21*` + host number + `#`.
- **Turn a rule off:** `#61#`, `#67#`, or `#21#` respectively.

On a Telstra business landline or a hosted PBX, the same options usually live in your online account or admin portal under "call forwarding" or "diversions" — often easier than dial codes.

## Optus (and most other carriers)

The GSM forwarding codes are an industry standard, so Optus, Vodafone, and most MVNOs use the same syntax as above:

- **No answer:** `*61*` + host number + `#`
- **Busy:** `*67*` + host number + `#`
- **All calls:** `*21*` + host number + `#`

If the codes don't take, your carrier's app or web account will have a call-forwarding toggle. Search their help centre for "call diversion."

## VoIP / hosted phone systems

If your venue runs on a VoIP or hosted system — common ones include Aircall, 3CX, RingCentral, MyNetFone/Vonex, or a system your POS or telco provider set up — forwarding is done in the admin dashboard, not with dial codes. Look for:

- **"Call routing," "call flows," or "forwarding rules."**
- An option to forward **on no-answer** with a ring timeout (e.g. forward after 4 rings / ~20 seconds).
- The ability to set **business-hours vs after-hours** routing if you want time-based rules.

Point the rule at the AI host number and save. If you use a ring group, add the host as the final destination so it catches anything the group doesn't.

## A quick test before you rely on it

Once forwarding is set, test it the way a guest would:

1. From a different phone, call your venue number and **let it ring out** (or call after hours).
2. Confirm the host picks up and sounds right.
3. Make a test booking and check it lands in your dashboard.
4. Make sure a normal answered call still works as usual.

Five minutes of testing saves any "is it actually working?" doubt later.
