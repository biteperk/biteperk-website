If you've started looking into an AI host for your restaurant's phone, you've probably noticed the pricing is all over the place. Some services quote a few dollars a month, others want hundreds, and a few won't give you a number until you've sat through a sales call. Here's how the pricing actually works, in plain terms, so you can tell a fair deal from an expensive one.

## The three pricing models you'll see

Almost every AI phone service is built on one of these:

- **Per-minute or per-call.** You pay for each call or each minute the AI spends on the phone. It looks cheap on the brochure, but the bill swings with how busy you are — and your busiest months, when the phone never stops, are exactly when it costs the most.
- **Flat monthly.** One predictable price, unlimited (or very high) call volume included. Easier to budget, and it doesn't punish you for being popular.
- **Per-cover or per-booking.** A cut of every reservation the system takes. Common with booking platforms, and it quietly adds up — a few dollars a cover across a full service is real money.

For most venues, a **flat monthly** price is the one that won't surprise you. A busy Friday shouldn't cost more than a quiet Tuesday just because more people called.

## What's a fair number in Australia?

As a rough 2026 benchmark for a single venue, a capable AI phone host lands somewhere between **$80 and $300 a month**, depending on features (booking integration, multiple locations, after-hours coverage). Below about $50 you're usually looking at a generic call bot, not something trained for hospitality. Well above $300 for one venue and you're paying for enterprise extras you may not need.

For comparison, that's a fraction of a single casual shift. The honest framing isn't "how cheap is it" — it's "what does one missed booking a night cost me instead?"

## The fees that hide in the fine print

Before you sign anything, ask about:

- **Setup or onboarding fees.** Some charge hundreds to get started.
- **Per-minute overage.** "Unlimited" that quietly caps at a number, then bills per minute after.
- **Integration add-ons.** Connecting your booking system or POS as a paid extra.
- **Lock-in contracts.** Month-to-month is a good sign a provider trusts its own product.

A clear, all-in monthly price with no per-call meter is the gold standard.

## How to work out if it pays for itself

This is the only sum that matters. Take your missed-call estimate — if you haven't done it, our guide on [what missed calls really cost your restaurant](/blog/what-missed-calls-cost-your-restaurant/) walks you through it in five minutes — and compare it to the monthly fee.

The arithmetic is usually lopsided. If an AI host recovers even **one or two bookings a week** that would otherwise have rung out, it has almost certainly paid for a whole month. Everything after that is margin you were previously leaving on the table.
