The UK hospitality year is not flat. It has a handful of days that carry the rest — Mother's Day, Valentine's, the run of Christmas parties through December, Sunday lunch every week — and they share one awkward feature: the demand arrives, in a rush, down the phone. The busiest booking days of your year are also the days your phone is least likely to be answered.

## Why the peaks hit the phone hardest

Two curves peak on the same day. Diners do their booking for a big occasion by calling — a Christmas party for eighteen with a set menu, a Mother's Day table for a family who want it just right, is not a two-tap online job, so people ring. At the same time your floor is at its fullest and your team is at its most stretched. The result is a spike in inbound calls landing precisely when nobody can pick up.

The cost of a missed call is higher on these days, too. A missed Tuesday two-top is a small loss. A missed booking for a twelve-cover Christmas party, or a Mother's Day family that books elsewhere, is a large one — and there are only so many of those days in the year to make up the difference.

## The trap of staffing for the peak

The instinct is to throw people at it: put someone on the phone for December, roster extra hands for Mother's Day. It helps, but it has two problems. You are paying for cover on your most expensive days, and one extra person still cannot answer three calls at once when the rush lands. You end up buying partial cover for the exact moment when partial is not enough.

## A calmer way through the season

The days that make your year should not depend on who happens to be near the phone when it rings. Three principles help.

- **Answer everything, instantly, in parallel.** The single biggest lever is making sure no peak-day call rings out — including the second and third calls that arrive at the same moment.
- **Capture the whole enquiry, not a callback.** A big-occasion booking has details: numbers, dietaries, a set menu, a deposit conversation. Capturing all of it on the first call beats a voicemail you return once the rush has passed and the caller has moved on.
- **Free your best people for the room.** On your busiest days you want your most experienced staff greeting guests and running the floor, not tied to a handset.
