The honest way to decide between hiring someone to cover the phone and using an AI host is to price both properly — not the headline wage, the real cost of an hour of reliable cover. Once you do, the comparison is less about money than about what you can actually staff.

## What an hour of cover really costs

From April 2026 the National Living Wage in the UK is **£12.71 an hour** for workers aged 21 and over ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/national-minimum-wage-rates)). That is the floor, not the true cost. On top of the wage sit employer National Insurance, pension contributions, holiday pay, training, and the simple fact that no one person covers every shift — you are staffing a rota, not a chair.

Add those and the fully-loaded cost of an hour of front-of-house cover runs well above the bare rate. For a role that has to be present across every service a phone might ring in — evenings, weekends, Sunday lunch — the hours stack up quickly, and they are the most expensive and hardest-to-fill hours you have.

## The part the maths hides

Even if you pay for that cover, you have not solved the problem, for three reasons.

- **One person, one call.** A human on the phone can take one call at a time. When three ring at once during the rush — which is exactly when they do — two of them still hit the engaged tone.
- **The phone is never the only job.** In a real venue the person nearest the phone is also seating guests, running food or working the pass. The phone loses that contest every time it matters most.
- **You are hiring for the peaks.** Phone demand is spiky. Staffing enough people to never miss a call at 8pm on a Saturday means paying them through quiet Tuesday afternoons too.

So the choice is not really "a person or a machine". It is "pay for cover you still cannot guarantee at the peak, or answer every call automatically and keep your people on the floor".

## What an AI phone host changes

An AI host answers every call at once, on the first ring, at any hour, and it does not get pulled away to run a table. It checks your real diary before it promises anything, books or takes the enquiry, and hands over to a person the moment a conversation stops being routine. Your team stays where the money is — in the room, with the guests in front of them.

That is not a reason to stop hiring good people. It is a reason to stop asking your good people to also be a switchboard during the busiest hour of their shift.

## Where each option genuinely wins

- **Hire a person** when the phone role is really a host role — greeting, atmosphere, upselling in the room — and the calls are a small, quiet part of it.
- **Use an AI host** when the calls cluster into service, when concurrency matters, when you are open long hours, or when the person who would answer is needed on the floor. Most UK restaurants and pubs are in this second case for at least their busiest shifts.

## How to price it for your venue

Take your busiest week. Count the hours you would need someone reachable purely for the phone, apply your fully-loaded hourly cost, and set that against what those answered calls are worth in covers. Then look at the calls that still ring out even with someone rostered. The gap between "cover you paid for" and "calls actually answered" is the number an AI host is really competing with.
