A no-show is worse than a missed call. A missed call is a booking you never had; a no-show is a table you turned away other guests for, held all night, and then watched sit empty. On a full Saturday, a couple of no-shows is a real dent in the night's takings — and unlike the weather, it is partly in your control.

The phone, used well, is one of the best tools for cutting them. Here is how, and where it stays on the right side of what diners will accept.

## Why bookings become no-shows

Most no-shows are not malicious. They are forgotten, double-booked, or made on a whim days earlier and never firmed up. The gap between "I booked somewhere for Friday" and "I am definitely going to that specific restaurant" is where no-shows live. Anything that closes that gap — a reminder, a confirmation, a small commitment — pulls the booking back to solid.

## What actually reduces no-shows

- **A confirmation the guest actively responds to.** A booking the guest has re-confirmed, even with a single word, is far more likely to turn up than one made and never touched again. The act of confirming is itself the commitment.
- **A reminder close enough to the day.** A nudge the day before catches the double-bookings and the forgotten diaries while there is still time to release the table or let a waitlist know.
- **A deposit or card on file for larger or higher-risk bookings.** Big groups, peak nights and set menus carry the most exposure; a deposit turns a casual booking into a committed one. Most diners accept this for exactly those situations, where they understand the venue's risk.
- **An easy way to cancel.** Counter-intuitively, making it simple to cancel reduces no-shows: a guest who can release a table they no longer need does so, and you get the night back to re-sell instead of holding it for someone who was never coming.

The balance to strike is commitment without friction. Push too hard — deposits on every two-top, aggressive reminders — and you deter the booking in the first place. Push too little and the table sits empty. The sweet spot is light-touch confirmation for everyday bookings and firmer commitment for the high-risk ones.

## Where the phone comes in

Reminders by text and email work, but they are easy to ignore. A short, warm confirmation call lands differently — it reaches the people who do not read restaurant emails, it lets the guest adjust numbers or timing on the spot, and it is a human-feeling touch that reminds them why they booked you.
